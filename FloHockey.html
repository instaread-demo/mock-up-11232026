What The Detroit Red Wings Are Getting In Michael Hage

Michael Hage was the centerpiece of the Detroit Red Wings trade return for Dylan Larkin. Chris Peters breaks down his game and how he fits for Detroit.

Nov 23, 2026 by Chris Peters

One of the other most anticipated trades finally happened as the Detroit Red Wings sent former captain Dylan Larkin to the Montreal Canadiens for a blockbuster package that included roster players Kirby Dach and Samuel Montembault, a series of draft picks including two first-round selections, and Montreal top prospect Michael Hage.

It’s that last piece of the deal that probably deserves the most oxygen aside from Montreal landing a clear No. 2 center behind Nick Suzuki to offer a matchups nightmare for opposing teams and another two-way player that can produce.

Peters says Detroit's own trade post shows how central Hage was.

Detroit listed Hage ahead of both NHL regulars, and Peters calls him the piece that sealed the deal.

The 20-year-old from Mississauga, Ontario, leads the NCAA with 26 points in 13 games at the University of Michigan.

Montreal drafted him 21st overall in 2024, and he has improved every year since into a potential top-six NHL scorer.

At 6-foot-1 he still needs to fill out, but Peters rates his hockey sense, skating and hands as high-end.

He surprised some by returning to Michigan for his junior year, as he was not yet ready to help Montreal.

Peters has followed him for five years, with the USHL's Chicago Steel and at Michigan.

Strengths

Hage scores everywhere: 10 points in 13 games as a Chicago rookie, then 75 points and 33 goals in his draft year replacing Macklin Celebrini.

He was a point-per-game freshman, had 52 points last season and 15 for Canada's bronze-medal World Juniors team. Peters says no college pace has matched his since Johnny Gaudreau's 80-point Hobey Baker season at Boston College.

Peters rates his offensive sense near elite, with NHL-speed reads and strong playmaking under pressure.

His skating now drives transition, and soft hands help him keep pucks. Peters prefers him as a playmaker.

Weaknesses

His lanky frame limits his physical bite in puck battles, though he is trending toward about 200 pounds.

His defensive detail has improved but remains limited for a top-six NHL center, Peters says.

So Peters wonders if Hage ends up on the wing, which he says is fine for a top-six play driver.

How He Fits In Detroit

Peters says Detroit has drafted well but lacked offensive upside, and Hage jumps to or near the top of its prospect pool.

With Larkin gone and Nate Danielson back in the AHL, Hage could play late this season and make next year's opening-night roster.

Peters believes his tools outweigh the flaws pros will test.

One caveat: Hage could return to school and reach free agency a year later, leverage Peters says could earn assurances about Detroit's plans.

Final Verdict

Detroit missed out on immediate help, but Peters says it landed a blue-chip prospect close to home and on a shorter NHL track, and fans should be excited.
