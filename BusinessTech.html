Woolworths in hot water

Bloomberg·23 Nov 2026
Listen to audio version of this article
0:00 / 0:00 Instaread
Sponsored by

Three of South Africa’s best-known companies will face challenges on issues including their pay policies and the independence of some of their directors at annual general meetings over the next week.

Proxy advisory services have recommended voting down the pay policy for the new chief executive officer of the food and clothing retailer Woolworths Holdings because it’s too generous.

A number of major US and Norwegian investors have indicated they will also oppose some pay resolutions.

Investment and medical insurance company Momentum Group faces opposition to its remuneration policy design.

Some Remgro investors say long-serving directors are no longer independent.

It is the first AGM season since Companies Act changes made pay votes binding. Median CEO pay rose 9% last year, PwC says.

ISS, the top proxy adviser, says CEO Sam Ngumeni’s incentives are too generous, a 15% rise for the finance director is unexplained, non-executive fees are too high and the committee’s discretion is too broad under a policy binding for three years.

Woolworths said the discretion clause is longstanding and unchanged.

ISS and Glass Lewis & Co. call Momentum’s policy poorly designed, and Glass Lewis faulted a one-off retention payment.

Florida’s SBA will oppose both companies’ pay policies; Storebrand ASA, Norges Bank Investment Management and CalSTRS will also vote against Woolworths.

Woolworths says pay is largely performance-based, with half of Ngumeni’s outperformance award tied to share-price growth.

The votes may still pass: almost 80% backed Woolworths’ policy last year, and Momentum faced little dissent in 2025.

Momentum’s Njabulo Nyawo said the retention scheme targets leadership continuity, with four of 14 executives near retirement, and pays only if targets are beaten through fiscal 2027.

No major company has lost a pay vote this season, though AVI drew 47% opposition.

Big funds will oppose re-electing long-serving Remgro directors, including chairman Johann Rupert, whom ISS also opposes, though special voting shares held by a Rupert-controlled company make the protest symbolic. Remgro did not immediately comment.

Shareholders of all three companies cast their votes at the annual general meetings scheduled over the coming week.
