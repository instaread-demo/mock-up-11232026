Likelihood of major US strikes on Iran rises as regional hostilities intensify

Western officials tell Bloomberg fresh military offensive on Iran becoming increasingly probable due to ongoing skirmishes. Trump faces European and Arab pressure to withhold fire, but Qatari mediation has failed so far.

by  Erez Linn —Published on  11-19-2026 15:06 —Last modified: 11-19-2026 15:38

Escalating hostilities have raised the likelihood of renewed mass US strikes on Iran, Western officials told Bloomberg on Thursday, though President Donald Trump has yet to decide whether to order attacks despite domestic post-midterm pressure for diplomacy.

Iran has hit US sites in Bahrain and Kuwait and Strait of Hormuz shipping, prompting a US strike on Qeshm island, and Houthi fire damaged a Saudi wealth fund tower. The New York Times says Trump is weighing a four-to-seven-day air campaign sought by Netanyahu.

Netanyahu backs force, a source said, as he works to keep Gadi Eisenkot from power. Becca Wasser and Dina Esfandiary of Bloomberg Economics say both sides are preparing for more fighting.

Western officials say striking risks reprisals on Arab allies and holding back cedes the strait. Brent crude is at $92.60, up over 50% this year.

Qatari mediation in Oman has stalled over sanctions, the blockade driving 90% inflation and nuclear demands.

Trump told Fox News he would see what happens after January 1.
