The couple bought a mansion, supercars, and luxury trips from their fraud proceeds.

A former Eden Prairie couple have been found guilty of wire fraud and money laundering in a scheme totaling $15 million that targeted Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers.

The U.S. Attorney’s Office of Minnesota announced the convictions this week of Gabriel Luthor, 40, and Elizabeth Brown, 43, who were each found guilty of six counts of wire fraud and one of money laundering following an 11-day jury trial.

Prosecutors said the couple used their neurofeedback therapy firm, Golden Victory Medical (GVM), to bill insurers falsely.

Claims covered care never given, unlicensed technicians’ work billed under licensed names, and padded visit times, including 586 claims of over 24 hours with patients.

The more than $15 million funded an Eden Prairie mansion, luxury vehicles, Vail and Miami trips, and nightclub tabs topping $10,000.

About $350,000 was also moved through bank accounts into a water well drilling venture in the DRC (Democratic Republic of the Congo).

The scheme began in 2018 in Las Vegas; Brown held a nurse practitioner license, while Luthor had no medical background.

GVM booked elderly, often homebound patients for home visits by unlicensed technicians, billing other services since neurofeedback is rarely covered.

Skipping copays, against Medicare rules, kept many patients from noticing the bills.

After a 2019 move to Minnesota, GVM was run from their 9,000 sq. ft. Eden Prairie mansion and expanded to Oklahoma, Kansas and Florida.

They ignored years of warnings, including nursing board complaints in Nevada and Minnesota.

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services later suspended payments after an auditor found support for just 9% of GVM’s billing codes.

The couple fired a CEO who warned of legal risk and an outside billing contractor who flagged overbilling.

U.S. Attorney Daniel N. Rosen said they treated elderly patients “like an ATM.”
